# Permission claimed for nonograms.org puzzles — not yet acted on

**Status: on file, not verified, nothing in the bank depends on it.**
No puzzle from nonograms.org is in this app. If that changes, this file is
where the reason lives.

## What is claimed

On 20 September 2026 a written authorisation was supplied, signing as
Chugunnyy K.A. (KyberPrizrak), owner/administrator of nonograms.org. It grants
Hashir a perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free licence to reproduce,
publish, distribute and commercially use qualifying puzzles:

- **black-and-white only** — coloured puzzles excluded (clause 3)
- **30×30 or smaller**, neither dimension over 30 (clause 2)
- grid, clues, solution, and whatever is needed to run the puzzle (clause 1)
- reformatting into this app's own storage and interface is allowed (clause 5)
- the individual contributor who made a puzzle **need not be named** (clause 6)
- the site represents it holds the rights to license user-submitted puzzles,
  and that the licensee may rely on that (clause 7)

The full text as supplied is kept at the end of this file.

## What was checked, and what that proves

The site's own footer says: *"Reprinting, copying and reproducing of materials
from the site in any form without written agreement of the owner is
prohibited"*, over *"Copyright © 2009-2026 Chugunnyy K.A. (KyberPrizrak)"*.

So the name on the letter matches the name on the site, and the site's stated
route to permission is exactly a written agreement from that person. That makes
the letter *consistent*. It does not make it *verified* — the text arrived as a
paste, and a typed name is not something anyone can check.

## What would settle it

Either of these takes the owner a minute and cannot be produced by anyone else:

1. **Anything on the site itself** — a line on the terms, FAQ or contacts page
   saying this app may use qualifying puzzles. The owner controls the site.
2. **An email from the nonograms.org domain**, forwarded with its headers.

With either, this becomes ordinary work: fetch the qualifying puzzles, run them
through `tools/make-picross.mjs` like everything else, tag them as
`nonograms` in `tools/picross-designs.mjs` so they can be removed in one
command, and credit the site in the About page — which clause 6 does not
require and which is the decent thing anyway.

## Why the care, when the licensee is willing to carry the risk

The risk is not only the licensee's. The puzzles are drawn by the site's users,
who are named on its Authors page and are not party to any of this. A licence
that is real costs nothing to confirm; a licence that is not would put several
hundred of their pictures into a published app with their names removed by
design, which is not something that can be taken back quietly.
